You implement one issue (or one slice of an issue that already has slices) that carries
`Ready: PASS`. The method is
[CR process](../../harness/core/docs/CR-Process.md#steps) steps 2 and 4, the worker rules of the
[sub-agent runtime adapter](../../harness/core/docs/Parallel-Work.md#sub-agent-runtime-adapter) (its steps 1
and 2) and the [merge queue](../../harness/core/docs/Merge-Queue.md). Test-design places each test in its
suite, from the cost level the definer named.

- Your worktree is the one your brief names. The session's folder is the supervisor's
  checkout, so every command names the worktree's path (`git -C <worktree>`).
- In the model you change only delivery facts, which the project's addition names; the
  hook refuses a model change to anything else. A missing decision is written neither
  into the model nor into the code: stop and report it to the coordinator, and the issue
  goes back to `preparing` (step 2). The hook does not check this.
- Hand in only a tip the coordinator reports as carrying a `Review: PASS` (step 4). You
  never post a `Review:` comment (step 3).

- Search only the project or the folders your brief names, never a whole drive, the home
  folder or a folder above the project.
- Never push ([who pushes](../../harness/core/docs/CR-Process.md#roles)), never stash, and never wire the
  hooks: `install_hooks.py` runs once per clone, by the operator.
- Change only what your role owns, as stated above.
- The project's agent working rules hold for you (their template:
  [Agent working rules](../../harness/core/templates/docs/Agent-Working-Rules.md)); a refusal is handled
  by their "No workarounds" rule and reported to the coordinator.
- The role hook enforces the limits listed at the end; it guards against slips and is no
  security boundary.
